## 1. Ownership, funding history, M&A, leadership changes

### Takeaway
StockTwits is still a privately held, venture-backed company. Its founder, Howard Lindzon, has run it again as CEO since late January or early February 2024, after Rishi Khanna's 2020–2024 tenure. I found no priced funding round after the December 2021 round: $30M led by Alameda Research Ventures, the venture arm of SBF's FTX-linked trading firm, at a reported $210M valuation. Since 2024 the company has gone smaller and more focused. It sold the brokerage accounts to Public for under $10M, bought a small AI startup (Thematic, July 2025), and runs at roughly 84 employees by one third-party count. I found no reports of layoffs or sale talks.

That stake presumably sits with the FTX bankruptcy estate or a buyer of its assets, which could complicate any future sale or recap. This is inference; I did not verify it.

## 2. How StockTwits makes money

### Takeaway
StockTwits has not disclosed revenue since 2013. It earns money from five sources:
- **sponsorships and advertising**, increasingly sold as branded "official partner" integrations to crypto exchanges, ETF issuers and prediction markets;
- **data licensing and API access**, its original business, which was re-emphasized in 2024;
- **consumer subscriptions**: Plus (ad-free) and Edge (premium);
- **events**: Stocktoberfest and the Cashtag Awards, both sponsored;
- **affiliate or lead-gen integrations** with brokers and exchanges.

The in-house brokerage, including crypto through FTX, launched in 2019 to 2022 and was shut by selling the accounts to Public in April 2024. Third-party revenue estimates cluster around **$5–25M per year**, with low confidence.

### Inferences
- The center of gravity has moved from transaction revenue (brokerage) to **paid presence**: "official partner" integrations, title sponsorships, sponsored video, sponsored watchlists, IR "premium message placement," and broker or exchange buttons on ticker pages. This is the clearest contrast with TickerHall's "no one pays to be seen." StockTwits' sponsors include leveraged-ETF issuers, crypto exchanges, prediction markets and microcaps (ZenaTech, REAlloys). The firms with the strongest incentive to buy attention are exactly the ones present.
- Data licensing is the most defensible revenue line: 17 years of cashtag-tagged, self-labeled bullish/bearish history that a new entrant cannot replicate. It is also the line the company is trying to reposition for AI and LLM buyers.
- At roughly $8/month (Plus) and $23–30/month (Edge), subscriptions are probably a minor revenue line relative to sponsorship and data. There is no evidence on subscriber counts.
- If revenue is roughly $5–25M with about 84 staff, the business is small and likely near breakeven. Ad and sponsorship demand from ETF, crypto and broker marketers tends to be cyclical with retail trading volumes (see Section 5).

### Gaps
- I found no reported revenue, revenue mix, subscriber count or ARPU for any year after 2013.
- I found no current list of data-licensing customers, and no confirmation of Bloomberg, Refinitiv/LSEG or any hedge fund as a current buyer. Bloomberg and Google as data clients is a 2013 fact.
- I found no public ad rate card, and no documentation of how sponsored posts or "premium placement" are labeled in feeds today.
- I could not confirm whether any LLM or AI-lab data-licensing deal has been signed.

## 3. User metrics and trend, 2021 to 2026

### Takeaway
The company's headline figure has risen steadily: 6M users at end-2021, 8M "active" in April 2024, and "10M+" since January 2025. It is a cumulative registered-user count, and the company labels it inconsistently as "registered," "unique" or "active." The only MAU figure ever disclosed is about 1M in December 2021, about 17% of registrations at the meme-stock peak. Third-party web-traffic estimates fell during 2025 and 2026: Semrush showed about 26.5M visits in January 2025, and Similarweb showed about 10–11M visits per month in April and September 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Company-reported user counts [CO]**
- 2013: about 230K active members. 2020: about 3M. The 2021 homepage dashboard showed 5M. Wikipedia's infobox says "8M+ registered." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StockTwits)
- **Dec 2021: 6M users (+50% in 2021), of whom 1M were MAU**, per CEO Rishi Khanna, who attributed the growth to the meme-stock surge. — [Bloomberg Law, Dec 16 2021](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/social-media-platform-stocktwits-nabs-210-million-valuation)
- Dec 2021 demographics: more than 75% of users male and under 45; average time spent of 30–120 minutes per day. — [The Daily Upside](https://www.thedailyupside.com/technology/stocktwits-social-media-platform-for-online-investors-is-worth-210-million/)

## 4. Product and strategy moves, 2024 to 2026

### Takeaway
Under Lindzon, StockTwits has done several things since 2024:
- exited brokerage and recommitted to "community and real-time discussion around earnings";
- bought an AI startup and shipped AI summaries, AI search and a "Social Relative Strength" index;
- embedded prediction markets (Polymarket) and futures data (CME), and launched Cryptotwits;
- relaunched its core symbol pages around "community intelligence," including people-based "watchers" metrics;

## 5. Strategic weaknesses visible from the business side

### Takeaway
StockTwits' real strengths are a 17-year data archive, ownership of the "cashtag" habit, roughly 10M registered accounts, a loyal US core audience with long sessions, and an events and partner network. Its weaknesses are structural:
- small scale (about $5–25M estimated revenue, about 84 staff);
- no new disclosed capital since an FTX-linked 2021 round;
- web traffic falling in 2025–26 and an undisclosed MAU;
- a failed brokerage pivot;
- growing dependence on sponsors (leveraged ETFs, crypto exchanges, prediction markets, microcaps) whose interest rises and falls with retail speculation.

The last point puts paid visibility directly into the product, which is the opening TickerHall's "no one pays to be seen" targets.

### Cited Findings

Similarweb shows about 10.4M in Apr 2026 and about 11M in Sep 2026, down 12.89% month over month. ### Inferences
- **Monetization pressure drives promotion.** A business of roughly $10M revenue that has stopped raising capital and exited transaction revenue has to sell attention. Its most motivated buyers (leveraged ETF issuers, crypto venues, prediction markets, microcap IR) want to be seen on exactly the tickers retail is watching. This is the structural reason a "no paid visibility" rival could win on trust, though only if it can fund itself in other ways.
- **Low-volatility exposure.** Traffic, and therefore ad, sponsor and Edge demand, falls when retail speculation cools, as the 2025–26 traffic decline shows. A new entrant launching in a quiet market faces the same headwind.
- **Thin team, broad surface.** About 84 people supporting web, apps, Cryptotwits, AI features, news, video, newsletters, events and a dozen data integrations suggests limited capacity for deep moderation or anti-spam. TickerHall's anti-gaming design (one vote per person, recorded calls) would target this, but this is an inference with no source on moderation staffing.
- **A real but beatable moat.** The cashtag network effect and data archive are durable, and new entrants cannot copy history. The live attention base (about 10–11M monthly web visits, an unknown number of app users) is modest. Retail attention has shifted to X (Twitter cashtags), Reddit, Discord and TradingView. StockTwits is an incumbent in a niche, not a dominant platform.
