# AI-Native Finance Assistants and the Retail Social-Sentiment Data Market (as of October 2026)

Scope note: Researched October 21, 2026. Many sources are vendor pages, press releases, or third-party review blogs; reliability is flagged inline. Dates are given where known. "Estimate" labels mark figures that are not company-reported.

## Q1. AI finance assistants for retail: what exists, do any use social sentiment, and how much are they used?

### Takeaway
By October 2026 every major AI and broker platform ships a stock-research assistant (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google Finance/Gemini, Claude, Robinhood Cortex, Public Alpha, Fiscal.ai). They take over the "what's happening with this ticker and why" job, but none of the big ones natively surfaces StockTwits- or Reddit-style crowd sentiment. Crowd sentiment reaches LLMs only through MCP connectors, and **StockTwits already runs an official MCP server for Claude, ChatGPT and other agents**, so it already sits in the slot TickerHall plans to fill.

### Inferences
- **(a) Is AI replacing the reason people visit stock-chat apps?** Partly. AI assistants now cover the informational job for free inside tools people already use: "why is $X moving", earnings summaries, screening, and portfolio Q&A (ChatGPT Finances now reaches US free users, Google Finance is global, Cortex has more than 1M users). They do not provide real-time peer chatter, community identity, entertainment, or a running public record of who said what. Those social and identity jobs are the defensible reason to visit a stock-chat app, so TickerHall should compete on them, not on information.
- The "sentiment into LLMs" distribution channel is real but already taken. StockTwits (official MCP across Claude and ChatGPT), Stockpulse (Claude directory connector), TipRanks (MCP with analyst ratings) and many scraper wrappers are live. A TickerHall MCP would launch late with a fraction of the message volume. Its only clear differentiator would be scored calls / verified track records, which StockTwits' MCP does not appear to expose.
- Big AI platforms license institutional data (FactSet, LSEG, PitchBook, S&P) rather than retail chatter, so a direct "Claude/ChatGPT licenses TickerHall data" deal looks unlikely. A user-installed MCP connector is the realistic route.

## Q2. Is there a paying market for retail social-sentiment data, who sells it, who buys it, and at what price?

### Takeaway
A paying market exists but it is small, fragmented and commoditizing. Institutional buyers get social sentiment mainly from large incumbents (ICE's Reddit product, LSEG MarketPsych, RavenPack, Context Analytics) and from StockTwits' own licensing. Developer and retail sentiment APIs sell for roughly $0–$600/month. The average alternative dataset has only about 20 investment clients. Academic evidence that crowd sentiment predicts returns is mixed and fragile, which caps what quants will pay.

### Cited Findings

**Market size (estimates vary widely by scope)**
- Neudata (buy-side-focused specialist, Feb 24, 2026): investment managers spent about $2.8bn on alternative data in 2025, up 17% YoY. Neudata projects about $23.1bn by 2030 "if current trends hold" (an implied about 50% CAGR, which looks inconsistent with 17% growth; verify against the full report). 57% of firms expect budgets to rise. Neudata tracked 2,805 datasets in 2025, up from 2,215 in 2024. **The average dataset has about 20 investment clients, down from 25 in 2024.** Web-scraped and transactional data take the largest share of spend, and social or sentiment data is not highlighted. — [Neudata: State of the alternative data market 2026](https://www.neudata.co/blog/state-of-the-alternative-data-market-2026)

**Academic evidence: does retail sentiment predict returns?**
- Bradley, Hanousek, Jame and Xiao, *Review of Financial Studies* (May 2024): WallStreetBets due-diligence posts predicted returns and cash-flow news before the GameStop squeeze, but that predictive power disappeared afterward, as posts shifted toward attention-grabbing stocks. An earlier working-paper version found about 1.1% two-day abnormal returns for buy recommendations over 2018–2020 and none for sells. — [EBSCO abstract](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/business-and-management/cf6a3620-b1a9-5907-94b9-b492bd0db320/place-your-bets-the-value-of-investment-research-on-reddit-s-wallstreetbets); [University of Kentucky scholars page](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/place-your-bets-the-value-of-investment-research-on-reddits-walls/)
- Leung, Wong and Wong (Cardiff/Edinburgh) studied about 30M StockTwits messages on 8,575 US firms (2010–2017). Sentiment significantly predicted returns, but less so for users who follow many stocks. In other words, focused users carry more signal, which supports a "who's been right" weighting. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsx05fv46nw5bkj7bu2o/portfolio/the-power-of-social-media-in-predicting-stock-returns); [Cardiff ORCA](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/150474)
- Swiss Finance Institute WP 21-33: StockTwits polarity does not, on average, predict next-day returns, but does predict abnormal returns around message-volume spikes. — [SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/n-21-33-event-studies-on-investor-sentiment)
- Al-Nasseri, Menla Ali and Tucker (2021, *IRFA*) used 289K StockTwits messages on DJIA 30 stocks. Sentiment matters mainly at extreme return quantiles. — [Brunel BURA](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/27154)
- An Oxford working paper finds WSB gives little or no price signal outside meme stocks such as GME and AMC. — [ORA Oxford](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:6f3c53d3-f0d6-4232-b3cf-bf1696b200f9/files/dsf268587w)
- Neudata (2026): 66% of buy-side firms use AI/LLMs mainly for productivity and only 31% to optimize strategies. AI has changed how data is consumed more than total vendor spend. — [Neudata 2026](https://www.neudata.co/blog/state-of-the-alternative-data-market-2026)

### Inferences
- **(b) Is there a paying market?** Yes, but it is thin for a newcomer. Institutional buyers want long, point-in-time histories (Estimize data goes back to 2011, StockTwits to about 2009, Reddit via ICE) and large message volumes for backtests. A pre-launch network has neither and may need 2–3 or more years of data before a quant fund will even trial it. With about 20 clients per dataset and about $80K per dataset per buyer (estimate), even a successful niche sentiment dataset is likely a low-single-digit-million revenue line at best.
- The low end (developer APIs, MCP access) is priced at about $0–$600/month and crowded with free or near-free alternatives (ApeWisdom, Swaggy, scraper MCPs). That points to commoditization of raw "bull/bear %" sentiment.
- Incumbents with scale (ICE with Reddit, LSEG MarketPsych, StockTwits with more than 10M users and an official MCP) already cover "aggregate retail sentiment". The most differentiated sellable asset is **scored, timestamped calls with verified track records**: per-user accuracy weighting. The academic hint is that focused or accurate users carry more signal (Leung et al.), and post-GME decay shows unweighted crowd signals degrade.
- Mixed academic evidence is a sales headwind. Buyers will demand out-of-sample proof, and that requires history TickerHall does not yet have.

- No independent confirmation of Stockpulse's institutional customers or of its exact current MCP pricing (the page would not render).
- Swaggy Stocks pricing or business model beyond a "free dashboard" was not found. HedgeFollow was not researched for lack of tool budget.
- Bloomberg's own social-sentiment product (older Twitter sentiment on the Terminal) is not confirmed for 2026. Only the Market Prophit CROWD index was found.
- No price was found for social-sentiment datasets specifically. Neudata figures cover all alt data.

## Q3. Do crowd "call scoring" and track-record products work commercially, and is there an MCP or LLM product for "who's been right"?

### Takeaway
Crowd forecast scoring and expert-accuracy ranking are proven niche businesses, not large ones. Estimize (crowdsourced EPS) was folded into ExtractAlpha in 2021 and sells through quant channels. TipRanks (accuracy-ranked analysts and bloggers) reached about a $200M valuation in 2024 after more than 10 years. No MCP server exposing verified retail stock-pick track records was found, which makes this a real open niche, though a small one.

### Cited Findings
- **Estimize**: ExtractAlpha (founded 2013 by Vinesh Jha) acquired it in 2021. Estimize crowdsources EPS and revenue forecasts from 100K–120K+ contributors (figures vary by source) across about 2,800 US equities, with daily data from about 2011 or 2012 (sources differ). It is distributed via WRDS (academia), QuantConnect, Dewey Data and, since December 2024, Interactive Brokers' trading platforms. ### Inferences
- Call scoring is TickerHall's most defensible data angle. Estimize and TipRanks show that institutions and retail will pay for accuracy-weighted crowd or expert signals, and no stock-focused "who's been right" MCP exists yet. The commercial ceiling is modest, though: TipRanks reached about $200M valuation and tens of millions in revenue (estimate) after about 12 years, while Estimize ended up inside a small quant-signal vendor rather than becoming a standalone large business.
- The pattern suggests track-record data makes money through (1) B2B white-label to brokers and banks (TipRanks' 50M partner reach) and (2) quant feeds via aggregators (Estimize via WRDS, QuantConnect and IBKR), more than through LLM or MCP queries. MCP is better treated as distribution and marketing than as a revenue line.
- StockTwits could add per-user accuracy to its MCP at low cost, so the window is open but could close quickly.

### Gaps
- Estimize pricing (institutional feed or retail) was not found.
- SumZero (buy-side idea-sharing with performance tracking) and Covestor (track-record-based copy portfolios) were not researched for lack of tool budget. Their business outcomes are unverified here.
- TipRanks' paid-subscriber count and audited revenue were not found. All revenue figures are third-party estimates.
- Whether the TipRanks MCP and ChatGPT app is operated by TipRanks itself is unconfirmed.
