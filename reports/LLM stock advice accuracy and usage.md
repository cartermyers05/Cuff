# Give Every AI the Receipts It Lacks

The gap is real, but it is narrower than "selling better market data into a person's LLM." What no AI assistant can do today is tell a trader **who has actually been right** on a ticker, and that missing ingredient, not market data in general, is TickerHall's opening. Frontier models get far better at factual market questions when they have tools: on time-sensitive lookups they score **0% without search**, and search adds about 40 points. Their stock picks, though, showed **no risk-adjusted edge** in the most rigorous live study (NBER, August 2025 to April 2026), and most of them lost money in live trading contests. Their answers rest on training data 4–21 months old, consumer explainer sites, corporate pages and unweighted chatter from Reddit, YouTube and X. Naive StockTwits sentiment calls next-day direction correctly **47.6%** of the time, while the same feed filtered to users with a record of being right hits **72.8%**. Demand is large: OpenAI says **200M+ people ask ChatGPT financial questions every month**, roughly a third of US investors have used AI for investing research, and **35.5%** of one 2026 sample have acted on an AI trade idea. Yet the assistant is the wrong place to look for revenue. ChatGPT bans selling subscriptions or upgrades in-chat, Claude has no payment system for connectors, about 1,000 Claude connectors compete for attention, and no finance vendor discloses revenue from the AI channel. StockTwits already runs a **live official MCP server** for Claude and ChatGPT that offers aggregate sentiment but no track records, alongside TipRanks, SentiSense, Unusual Whales and a Stocktwits connector built into Perplexity. TickerHall should therefore be the source, not the pipe. That means building the scored-call ledger so machines can read it, getting it cited by AI search, listing a free connector tied to a TickerHall account once the data is dense enough (around May 2027 rather than Q3 2027), proving its value with a pre-registered "with vs. without TickerHall" test, and earning money through Pro on tickerhall.com and B2B licensing. For you, the next 90 days hold four tasks inside the existing 8 TickerHall hours a week: an AI baseline audit, a benchmark protocol, an "AI callers" board in the private beta and a spec for a machine-readable ledger. None of it changes the PostDose split.

## Tools fix AI's facts, not its stock picks

The evidence splits cleanly. On questions with a checkable answer, LLMs improve sharply when they get the right data. On questions about what a stock will do, better models and more browsing have not produced a durable edge.

**Live trading is the harshest test, and most models failed it.** In nof1's Alpha Arena Season 1, six frontier models each traded $10,000 of real money in crypto perpetual futures for about 17 days from October 18, 2025, with identical prompts and no human input. Only the two Chinese models finished ahead: **Qwen3 Max +22.3%** and DeepSeek +4.89%. Claude Sonnet 4.5 lost 30.81%, Grok 4 lost 45.3%, Gemini 2.5 Pro lost 56.71% and **GPT-5 lost 62.66%** ([iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/)). Other outlets' final balances differ by a few points and put GPT-5 nearer −59% ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/amp)). Season 1.5 switched to tokenized US stocks from November 20, 2025. In a December 6 snapshot **only 1 of 8 models was profitable**, an unreleased Grok 4.20 at +22.38%, while Claude Sonnet 4.5 stood at −35.08% and Grok 4 at −53.39% ([Lookonchain](https://m.lookonchain.com/feeds/39476)). Grok 4.20 went on to win with a +12.11% aggregate return over about two weeks ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/ai-model-grok-4-2-triumphs-in-trading-tournament/)).

Data was not the only problem. The models received nothing but price series, and nof1's Jay Zhang said "LLMs do not handle numerical time series data well, but that was the entire context we provided them" ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/amp)). Season 1's winner made only about 43 trades, fewer than three a day ([iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/)), which points to position sizing, leverage and over-trading as the real killers. nof1 raised $15M and promised a Season 2 with web search ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/nof1-raises-15m-to-run-ai-models-in-live-trading)). No results had appeared by October 2026, so no live trading result yet compares models with and without search.

**Stock-pick studies show the same pattern under better controls.** NBER working paper w35153 prompted ChatGPT 5.0 and 5.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Flash and Grok 4.1 Fast every day from August 2025 to April 2026, for more than 1,200 portfolio-days. **All seven actively managed portfolios beat the S&P 500 on raw return, and the edge disappeared once returns were adjusted for size, value and momentum** ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35153); [Science of Money](https://www.scienceofmoney.org/when-chatbots-play-stock-picker-what-ai-actually-recommends-for-your-portfolio-559/)). The picks explain why. Semiconductors averaged **41%** of the portfolios against about 20% of the index, and ChatGPT 5.0 put 18–20% in Nvidia alone. The average beta was about 1.6, and the chosen stocks had about ten times the news coverage of the average listed firm. That is a leveraged bet on a tech rally, not skill. The Claude, Gemini and Grok samples also cover only about a month.

Other tests point the same way. StockBench is a benchmark built to avoid training-data contamination. It ran agents on the top 20 Dow stocks from March 3 to June 30, 2025, and they returned between −2.8% and +2.5%, against +0.4% for buy-and-hold, with drawdowns of 11–15% ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v1)). FINSABER is a 20-year test across 100+ symbols, accepted at KDD 2026. It found that previously reported LLM advantages "deteriorate significantly," with strategies too conservative in bull markets and too aggressive in bear markets ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v3)). Press coverage of it reported that a decades-old ARIMA statistical model beat the AI tools on a risk-adjusted basis ([Moneywise via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/think-outsmart-market-letting-ai-130000111.html)).

**Two mechanisms make positive backtests mislead.** The first is memorization. On one benchmark, DeepSeek 3.2's annualized alpha went from **+20.73% in-sample to −1.04% out-of-sample**; the vendor that reported this sells tools for avoiding the bias ([HedgeFundAlpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/education/your-llms-alpha-might-be-mere-memorization/)). A December 2025 paper found that memorization "substantially amplifies" apparent forecasting power, and that the effect loses significance after the training cutoff ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847v1)). The second is decay. Lopez-Lira and Tang's GPT-4 news signal is the flagship positive result in this literature. Its **Sharpe ratio fell from 6.54 in late 2021 to 1.22 in early 2024** as LLM adoption rose, and the strategy is unprofitable at 20 basis points of trading cost ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)).

As advisers, LLMs fall short too. In a King's College London study, the most conservative AI setup left clients with **18% less wealth after 20 years** than the average human adviser, without cutting volatility much ([KCL](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/ai-investment-advice-may-sacrifice-returns-without-reducing-risk)). In a separate study, web search made the models' advice more uniform across very different clients ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23837)).

**Factual accuracy is better, but still short of reliable.** FinanceBench (2023) showed that accuracy on filing questions depends almost entirely on how the evidence reaches the model. GPT-4-Turbo answered **19%** correctly with a shared document index, about 50% with a per-document index, 79% with the full filing in context and **85%** when handed the exact passage ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2311.11944v1); [Beancount](https://beancount.io/bean-labs/research-logs/2026/05/12/financebench-open-book-financial-qa-benchmark)). Vals AI's Finance Agent benchmark is the best current gauge of analyst-style work on filings with tools, but its figures conflict across sources until they are read by version and date:

Vals now marks v2 as superseded by v2.1. The headline "GPT-5.5 hits just 52%" refers to v2 ([KuCoin](https://kucoin.com/blog/can-ai-replace-financial-analysts-in-2026-vals-ai-finance-agent-v2-reveals-gpt-5-5-hits-just-52-percent-accuracy)). A vendor's June 2026 claim that "no model clears 58%" matches no version that can be verified ([Barebone AI](https://barebone.ai/resources/how-accurate-is-ai-stock-analysis)). Read together, the best tool-using agents get **roughly six in ten to two in three** entry-level analyst questions right. A harder version of the test knocks off a few points until the next model arrives.

Broader audits are worse. A September 2026 test of 17 current models on 121 money questions found **43%** average accuracy, falling to 12% on multi-step questions ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/fintech/ai-chatbots-give-wrong-financial-answers-most-of-the-time-study-finds/268267)). Columbia's Tow Center found that eight AI search engines mis-cited news **more than 60%** of the time; Perplexity was wrong least often (37%) and Grok-3 most often (94%) ([CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php)). An EBU/BBC review of more than 3,000 answers found **45%** had a significant issue ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/24/bbc_probe_ai_news/)). No independent audit of stock-quote accuracy exists.

**Search changes the numbers for lookups, not for judgment.** FinSearchComp, an ICLR 2026 benchmark of 635 expert-written questions, found that "models without search uniformly score 0" on time-sensitive data. Search added an average of **40.8 points** on those questions, 29.0 points on simple historical lookups and only 8.1 points on complex investigations ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13160)). In a like-for-like run, Gemini 2.5 Pro went from 10.0% without Google Search to 34.8% with it. The best search-enabled product, Grok 4 with web access, scored 68.9 against a human-expert baseline of 75.0 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13160)). StockBench found that richer inputs help trading only a little: Kimi-K2's return fell from 1.9% with all inputs to 1.4% without news, and to 0.6% without news or fundamentals ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v1)).

The measurable payoff from better data is in **answering questions**: with the right inputs, an assistant can state something true, current and sourced that it otherwise could not. Better trades have not been shown. That distinction decides how TickerHall should pitch its data.

## Today's AI stock answer rests on stale memory, explainer sites and unscored chatter

Without a tool call, a model answers from training data. Third-party compilations of training cutoffs disagree on some dates, but they list Gemini 3.1 Pro at January 2025, GPT-5.5 at December 2025, Grok 4.7 at May 2026 and Claude Opus 5.5 at June 2026 ([Temso](https://www.temso.ai/blog/ai-knowledge-cutoff-dates-every-major-llm-updated-for-2026); [FixAEO](https://fixaeo.com/ai-knowledge-cutoff/); [Metehan.ai](https://metehan.ai/articles/llm-knowledge-cutoff-dates/)). As of October 21, 2026, that is **4 to 21 months of staleness** before anyone asks about a price, an earnings result or this week's mood.

With search on, the sources are mostly generic. In Google AI Overviews' finance answers from July to September 2026, the most-cited domains were NerdWallet (cited in 17.8% of answers), YouTube (17.2%), Reddit (16.3%), Investopedia (15.4%) and CNBC (14.9%). Morningstar ranked **#84 at 0.7%**, and Seeking Alpha and Bloomberg missed the top 100 ([LLM Authority Index](https://llmauthorityindex.com/resources/research/top-100-google-ai-overviews-citations-investing-retirement-mortgage-financial-high-stakes-consumer-decisions)). Across all topics, Reddit supplies 6.6% of Perplexity's citations and Wikipedia 7.8% of ChatGPT's ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns)), and on finance Perplexity "leans harder into UGC [user-generated content] and affiliate content than any other model" ([Goodie](https://higoodie.com/blog/most-cited-finance-domains-ai-search/)). In the NBER study, chatbots visited about 16 websites per stock query, roughly two-thirds of them corporate sites ([Science of Money](https://www.scienceofmoney.org/when-chatbots-play-stock-picker-what-ai-actually-recommends-for-your-portfolio-559/)). None of these sources scores who has been right, and no study yet isolates citations for ticker prompts such as "Is NVDA a buy?"

**Built-in finance data has grown fast, but it is institutional, personal or raw.** ChatGPT search has shown stock charts since October 31, 2024, from data providers OpenAI does not name ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-search/)). ChatGPT Finances connects accounts through Plaid. Its rollout dates conflict across sources and reconcile as follows: a May 15, 2026 preview for US Pro subscribers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/15/openai-launches-chatgpt-for-personal-finance-will-let-you-connect-bank-accounts/)), extension to Plus in June, and **availability to US Free and Go users on October 2, 2026**, per OpenAI's release notes ([OpenAI release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/personal-finance-chatgpt/); [Eigen Radar](https://eigenradar.com/en/ai/briefs/2026-10-03/chatgpt-finances-free-go-access/)). It reads portfolios but carries no sentiment or crowd data. ChatGPT for Financial Services, launched September 10, 2026 for banks and research teams, hosts Daloopa, PitchBook and LSEG News on OpenAI's own infrastructure ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/data/openai-launches-chatgpt-for-financial-services-with-integrated-data-sources-it-pulls-research-cites-it-and-builds-decks-in-minutes)). Claude for Financial Services connects to S&P Capital IQ, Daloopa, Morningstar, PitchBook, LSEG live market data, Moody's, MT Newswires and Aiera, none of them a crowd source ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/advancing-claude-for-financial-services)). Perplexity offers bring-your-own-license connectors that include FactSet, Morningstar, Canary's insider signals and **Stocktwits "bullish/bearish signals"** ([Perplexity Help Center](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/12870803-premium-data-sources)). Google Finance added Kalshi and Polymarket prediction-market odds ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-google-finance-ai-deep-search/)), and Grok reads X posts natively ([xAI](https://docs.x.ai/cookbook/examples/sentiment_analysis_on_x)).

Track records barely appear. TipRanks' MCP server (June 3, 2026) exposes a rank and average one-year return for **sell-side analysts** ([TipRanks](https://mcp.tipranks.com/); [Glama](https://glama.ai/mcp/connectors/com.tipranks/tipranks)). StockTwits' official MCP server at mcp.stocktwits.com is live. An older, undated note from CEO Howard Lindzon described it as being in internal testing, but the live endpoint settles the question. It serves trending tickers, per-ticker bull/bear sentiment, message volume and individual messages to Claude, ChatGPT, Codex and Cursor. Its only advertised permissions are `read` and `watch_lists`, and it shows **no per-user accuracy** ([ai.stocktwits.com](https://ai.stocktwits.com/); [OAuth metadata](https://mcp.stocktwits.com/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource)). Its Claude and ChatGPT directory listings, linked from that page, could not be opened independently.

### Accuracy-weighted crowd data is the missing layer, on paper

The strongest number for your thesis comes from HKUST and IDEA Research (arXiv, v2 November 2025). Aggregated StockTwits sentiment predicted the next day's direction **47.63%** of the time, which the authors call "not better than random guessing." They then defined "experts" as users with ≥80% accuracy on their last 20 posts and ≥65% over two years. **Expert signals hit 72.8%**, a 25-point gap. On the NASDAQ-100, a strategy that bought the top tenth of stocks and sold the bottom tenth returned 32.91% a year at a Sharpe ratio of 2.69 after 4 basis points of costs ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10078)). Two other studies agree on the direction: social sentiment predicts less well when users spread themselves across many stocks ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3492722)), and self-labeled sentiment is "only slightly predictive in aggregate" while individual users differ sharply ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3532856)). The signal sits in **who** is talking. The assistants see only **what** is being said.

The counter-evidence is just as specific, and TickerHall should design around it. HKUST's experts covered only **about 4% of stock-days**, and reaching about 90% required spreading their signals across related stocks. The paper never fed those signals into a language model, and no published study measures the lift from adding sentiment or track-record data to one ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10078)). FINSABER shows that such backtest advantages shrink over longer windows ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v3)). Alpha Arena's losses came from behavior rather than missing data, and NBER's chatbots browsed about 16 sites per query and still showed no edge after adjusting for risk factors. Lopez-Lira and Tang show that an edge fades as more people use it ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)), so a "top callers" signal inside everyone's assistant would face the same pressure. And using ChatGPT outages as natural experiments, a 2026 study found that ChatGPT makes retail order flow **more correlated**, with a shared component that "shows little evidence of return predictability" ([He, AFA](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=257180)). A widely piped crowd signal could add to that herding.

Two implications follow. TickerHall's defensible offer is **accountability and answerability**: a verifiable answer to "who has been right on $X." Predictive lift is a hypothesis to test in public, not a promise. And because look-ahead bias is the field's main credibility problem, calls that are stamped when posted and can never be edited are a methodological asset that sophisticated buyers will value.

One more pattern works in TickerHall's favor (my inference). LLMs crowd into the same high-attention names, with semiconductors at 41% of picks and ten times the average news coverage. So TickerHall does not need broad coverage to matter in AI answers. Dense records on the few dozen names people ask about most would cover a large share of real queries.

My working estimate from these figures is that **roughly a third to a half of US investors have used a chatbot for investing research, and about 10–20% have used one for trade ideas or a final buy/sell decision**. Use skews heavily toward under-45s. In Betterment's 2026 survey, 48% of Gen Z investors say AI has already influenced a financial decision, against 5% of boomers ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/18/gen-z-ai-investing-advisors-financial-planning-sports-betting/)). The same cohort leans on social media: 61% of investors under 35 use finfluencer recommendations ([FINRA Foundation](https://www.finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2025-11/NFCS_Investor_Survey_Report_White_Paper.pdf)). Turning these shares into headcounts would need a figure for the total US investor population, which this research did not cover.

ChatGPT dominates the tool mix. Among AI-using investors in 2024, 66% used ChatGPT, 42% Gemini, 37% Copilot, 17% Perplexity and **15% Claude** ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/research/survey-how-investors-are-using-generative-ai)). No AI lab publishes how much of its traffic is about investing. OpenAI's 1.1-million-conversation usage study contains no finance or stock topic share ([OpenAI](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/a253471f-8260-40c6-a2cc-aa93fe9f142e/economic-research-chatgpt-usage-paper.pdf)), and Reuters noted that OpenAI "has not released data on how many people use ChatGPT to choose investments" ([Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/09/25/analysis-039chatgpt-what-stocks-should-i-buy039-ai-fuels-boom-in-robo-advisory-market--)).

Platform signals show the direction instead. Robinhood's Cortex Digests had reached nearly 1 million customers by April 2026 ([Robinhood](https://investors.robinhood.com/news-releases/news-release-details/robinhood-reports-first-quarter-2026-results)). On its July 2026 call, Robinhood said **more than 100,000 people** had opened agentic trading accounts; that figure comes from search summaries of the transcript ([Q2 2026 transcript](https://investors.robinhood.com/static-files/f44ea493-9c52-4744-9671-7720e1ac7b2f)). eToro launched connectors for Claude, ChatGPT and Grok on October 8, 2026, but **not for US clients** ([eToro](https://www.etoro.com/news-and-analysis/press-releases/your-ai-already-runs-your-day-now-it-knows-your-portfolio/)). Investing.com's WarrenAI assistant logged about 2.79 million interactions in 12 months ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/fintech/ai-investing-takes-hold-far-beyond-wall-street-new-data-shows/268478)).

**Trust is shallow, and that is the opening.** Among younger Vanguard investors, 63% report little or no trust in AI guidance ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/fintech/ai-financial-advice-lifts-demand-for-human-advisors-says-vanguard/268433)). The top worry in the Investing.com survey is incorrect recommendations (38.9%), and **24.2% worry about herding from shared AI signals** ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/blog/how-retail-investors-are-using-ai-in-2026-339)). The behavioral studies show why that worry is fair. When Italy temporarily banned ChatGPT in 2023, investors narrowed their trades toward popular assets ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5778246.pdf?abstractid=5778246&mirid=1)). When ChatGPT is available, retail order flow moves together without becoming more informative ([He, AFA](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=257180)).

For TickerHall, the target user is clear. They ask an assistant, half-believe the answer and check it somewhere else. A citable, dated record of who has been right is exactly what that checking step needs. The people who matter most are the roughly one in three AI users who have acted on an AI idea: young, active and already used to finfluencers.

Economically, the channel works more like search-engine optimization than an app store: listing is free, discovery depends on users asking for a brand by name, and the sale happens on your own site.

**The framing that wins is narrower.** TickerHall is *the public record of who has been right*: readable by people, cited by AI search and callable from any assistant. In one line: "Your AI can tell you what people think. TickerHall tells it who's been right." In that framing the LLM does three jobs. It distributes TickerHall through citations in AI answers and a free connector. It proves the data's worth through a published with-versus-without test. And it opens the B2B door, because platforms license proprietary derived data. The money itself comes from Pro sold on tickerhall.com and from licenses.

The main risk is unchanged from the earlier analysis. StockTwits could add a per-user accuracy tool to its live MCP within weeks, and could score its archive of tagged posts after the fact (my inference). TickerHall's defenses are a stricter method (no deletes, a fixed scoring window, returns measured against a benchmark, published rules), a neutrality StockTwits cannot claim while it sells sponsored placement, and starting the clock now, because history cannot be backfilled honestly.

### Publish a pre-registered "with vs. without TickerHall" test as the launch story

The test makes two claims, and only one of them is certain to come out well. **The first is answerability.** Ask ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok and Perplexity questions like "Who has been most accurate on $NVDA over the last 90 days?" Without TickerHall, no assistant can answer from a verified source, because no such source exists (my inference). With TickerHall, it can. This works like FinSearchComp's 0% on time-sensitive data without search. It is a factual demonstration rather than a performance claim, so it carries little regulatory risk.

**The second is predictive lift, and it must be tested honestly.** Every week, have each assistant make a bullish or bearish call on a fixed list of about 25 tickers: the most-asked megacaps plus that week's earnings reporters. Run four arms. Arm A is the assistant with web search alone. Arm B adds StockTwits' naive sentiment. Arm C adds a TickerHall summary weighted by caller accuracy, pasted into the prompt, so no MCP is needed yet. Arm D is the TickerHall consensus of top callers on its own. Score every call one week later against SPY, using the same rule human callers face. Because every call is scored after it is made, look-ahead bias is ruled out.

Publish the protocol before the beta starts, and publish the results at **≥300 scored calls per arm**, about 12 weeks at 25 tickers a week. At that size, a 10-point gap in hit rate is about 2.5 standard errors, and a gap the size of HKUST's 25 points would be unmistakable (my calculation). Publish the result even if it is null. "TickerHall's top callers beat naive StockTwits sentiment, inside your own AI" is the story you want. "Accuracy-weighted crowds didn't help an AI this quarter" still makes TickerHall the most credible voice in a category full of untested claims. Run Arm A in public as an **AI-callers board**: the assistants appear as labeled benchmark accounts, are scored by the same rules, stay off the human leaderboards and out of WAC, and have their prompt published.

### Regulatory guardrails for an "AI data" pitch

AI-washing is the most direct risk. The SEC fined Delphia $225,000 and Global Predictions $175,000 in March 2024 for false AI claims, charged Nate's founder in April 2025, and created a dedicated emerging-technologies enforcement unit in February 2025 ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/10/2025-fiscal-year-in-review-sec-enforcement-against-investment-advisers); [StoneTurn](https://stoneturn.com/insight/next-generation-compliance-preparing-for-continued-sec-ai-washing-enforcement/)). Never market "makes your AI pick better stocks" without published out-of-sample evidence. Describe accuracy only as computed by the published rule, and label any backtest as a backtest. Match StockTwits' disclaimer standard, which states the data is "for informational purposes only" and is not investment advice ([ai.stocktwits.com](https://ai.stocktwits.com/)).

Brokers that license TickerHall data will need an audit trail, because FINRA tells firms using generative-AI chat to supervise it and keep the sessions ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2025-01/2025-annual-regulatory-oversight-report.pdf)). An immutable ledger supplies exactly that. FINRA's December 2025 report on social-media investing noted that firms are "harness[ing] social media data for market analyses and product development," and its comment period closed in May 2026 ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/key-topics/fintech/report/social-media-influenced-investing)), so scoring identifiable public traders will draw attention.

Before the MCP and Pro ship, get three things in writing: counsel's view on whether paid alerts about top callers, delivered through an assistant, could make TickerHall an investment adviser or fall under the publisher's exclusion; a clause in the terms of service licensing users' calls for redistribution to AI tools and partners; and rights to redistribute through an API the price data used to score calls. Stay in the read-only lane, since both directories bar executing trades and serving ads.

## Conclusion

The most useful shift in thinking is this: in a market increasingly mediated by AI, the scarce input is **accountability**, not information. Assistants pull everyone toward the same megacaps and the same generic sources, and the evidence shows they make retail order flow move together. That makes an independent record of who has been right more valuable as more people ask the same few models. It also means any predictive edge in that record will decay once it is piped everywhere. So TickerHall should sell the record's trustworthiness rather than its alpha, and its moat is the clock: history stamped at posting accumulates, and nobody except StockTwits can credibly backfill it.

The thesis can also be tested quickly and cheaply. A pre-registered with-vs-without experiment, run inside the private beta and readable after about 12 weeks of scored calls, will show whether "data for your AI" is a headline or a business before anyone commits more hours. If accuracy-weighted calls lift an assistant's hit rate, TickerHall has its launch story, its B2B pitch and its reason to list everywhere. If they don't, TickerHall still owns the only honest answer to "who's been right," and that remains the product either way.
